# Visual Recipes

Use these recipes as visual grammar, not text to copy verbatim. Combine one primary recipe with only the compatible details required by the user's request.

## Game Item Icon

Design one unmistakable silhouette centered within a stable square composition. Use a slight three-quarter view when volume matters, controlled perspective, generous negative space, and separation between the object and background. Establish a bright focal edge, readable material cues, selective wear, and a restrained rarity accent. Keep the image legible at thumbnail size. Exclude extra objects, cropped edges, tiny labels, random symbols, clutter, and muddy shadows.

## Game Character Concept

Define role, body type, age range, silhouette, costume layers, equipment, pose, and expression before surface decoration. Use a camera angle that reveals the design, clear value groups, readable hands and feet, and material contrast between cloth, leather, metal, skin, and effects. Keep anatomy, equipment attachment, and left-right details coherent. Exclude duplicated limbs, fused props, floating accessories, inconsistent scale, and excessive micro-detail.

## Creature And Beast

Anchor the design in a believable skeletal structure, weight distribution, locomotion, and ecological role. Describe head shape, limbs, hide or scales, distinctive silhouette features, and controlled fantasy exaggeration. Use environmental scale cues and lighting that separates the creature from the scene. Exclude random horns, repeated eyes, decorative noise, broken joints, and weightless poses.

## Game Environment

Establish a primary landmark, traversable foreground, middle-ground route, distant depth, scale references, and atmosphere. Name time of day, weather, terrain, architecture, material age, and story traces. Use leading lines and value grouping to guide the eye toward the gameplay focal point. Exclude empty scenery, uniform detail, unclear scale, accidental tangencies, and a horizon that competes with the subject.

## UI Background And Frame

Separate decorative art from interaction zones. Define the safe center, edge framing, panel hierarchy, corner treatment, material language, and contrast reserve for native text and controls. Keep ornament density higher near borders and lower behind content. Use lighting and texture that support legibility rather than imitating clickable controls. Exclude baked-in placeholder text, fake buttons, tiny labels, unreadable ornament, and noisy central detail.

## App Or Web Mockup

Describe the product domain, information hierarchy, viewport, navigation pattern, primary workflow, density, component language, and realistic content blocks. Use consistent spacing, alignment, typography scale, and interaction states. Make the screen look usable rather than like a marketing collage. Exclude illegible microcopy, overlapping panels, contradictory navigation, excessive floating cards, and decorative elements that resemble controls.

## Poster And Key Art

Choose one dominant focal subject, supporting secondary elements, an intentional reading path, and a clear reserved typography zone. Specify crop, camera energy, atmosphere, palette, contrast, and print or screen finish. Use depth and lighting to unify rather than collage the elements. Exclude random text, multiple competing focal points, edge collisions, incoherent scale, and low-contrast title space.

## Product Hero Image

Specify exact product geometry, material, finish, viewing angle, lens character, surface, background, shadow behavior, and brand palette. Use controlled studio key, fill, rim, and reflections that describe the form without hiding edges. Preserve logos and labels exactly when supplied. Exclude warped packaging, invented branding, unreadable labels, duplicate products, dirty highlights, and floating contact shadows.

## Portrait

Define subject identity, age range, expression, gaze, wardrobe, grooming, pose, framing, focal length, depth of field, background context, and skin treatment. Use motivated key and fill light with restrained retouching and catchlights appropriate to the scene. Preserve facial identity when a reference is supplied. Exclude plastic skin, asymmetric eyes, malformed ears or hands, excessive sharpening, and unrelated accessories.

## Illustration

Choose one medium and rendering logic: editorial ink, gouache, watercolor, cel animation, painterly fantasy, paper cut, or another coherent treatment. Define line behavior, shape language, palette, texture, depth, and edge hierarchy. Adapt subject detail to the medium instead of stacking unrelated style labels. Exclude mixed rendering systems, uncontrolled texture, muddy values, and arbitrary decoration.

## Infographic

Define the communication goal, reading order, sections, diagram type, icon system, labels, units, and visual hierarchy. Reserve enough space for exact text and numeric values. Use a limited palette with semantic color assignments and strong contrast. Exclude invented data, illegible labels, decorative charts, inconsistent units, and unsupported conclusions.

## Reference Image Edit

List preserved properties first, then requested changes. Preserve geometry, identity, typography, layout, logo, palette, or camera when required. Describe the edit locally and keep untouched regions stable. Exclude global redesign, new text, rearranged elements, identity drift, altered proportions, and changes outside the requested area.

## Negative Constraint Selection

Use only defects plausible for the selected recipe. Common groups include malformed anatomy, duplicate elements, warped geometry, cropped focal subjects, unreadable or invented text, watermarks, clutter, inconsistent lighting, mismatched perspective, plastic materials, muddy contrast, oversaturation, halos, and compression artifacts. Do not append every group to every prompt.
